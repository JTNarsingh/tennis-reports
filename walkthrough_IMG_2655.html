THE FIX

Keep in mind

The whole report in six lines. If you remember nothing else, remember these.

  • Body turn. Chest to the side fence before the ball bounces — the turn takes the racket back, not the arm.
  • Racket drop. Let the racket head fall below the ball, then swing up through it.
  • Contact in front. Meet the ball in front - where you can still see it.
  • Contact height. Take the ball earlier, before it climbs past your waist.
  • Stance width. Feet wider than your shoulders before you swing.
  • Head stillness. Keep your eyes on the contact point until the ball is gone.
  • Balance at finish. Finish with your weight on the front foot, moving into the court.

Your practice plan

One drill at a time, ten balls at a time. Start at the top — the order matches the walkthrough. Film again when you have done a few sessions: anything you fix disappears from this page by itself.

Body turn

Strings to the side fence

  1. Stand sideways-on with no ball. Keep your feet still.
  2. Put your free hand on the racket throat and turn your chest until BOTH shoulders point at the side fence.
  3. You should be able to see the side fence through your strings.
  4. 3 sets of 20 slow turns. The arm never pulls the racket back - the turn takes it back.

Working when: In your next video, your shoulders look WIDE to the camera at the top of the turn.

10 turn-first forehands

  1. Have balls fed slowly, or drop-feed yourself.
  2. Only goal for all 10: chest fully turned to the side fence BEFORE the ball bounces.
  3. Do not care where the ball goes. The turn is the whole exercise.

Working when: The bounce happens and you are already turned, waiting.

Racket drop

10 brush-up forehands

  1. Drop-feed slowly. Before swinging forward, let the racket head fall until it is clearly BELOW the ball.
  2. Swing low-to-high and finish over your opposite shoulder.
  3. Target: every ball crosses the net at least 2 metres high, with arc. Height proves the brush; do not aim for lines.

Working when: The ball flies with visible arc and dips back into the court.

Swing OUT, not just up

  1. On the next 10, focus on swinging OUT to a contact point away from your body, wrist relaxed.
  2. Do not pull the ball close to you - the out-and-away reach is what lets the racket brush up naturally.
  3. If a ball comes low, bend LOWER with your legs instead of dropping only the racket.

Working when: Spin appears without feeling like you muscled it.

Contact in front

50 front-foot contacts

  1. Drop-feed easy balls. The only goal: meet every ball level with or ahead of your front foot.
  2. Freeze one beat after each hit and look - can you still see the contact point in front of you?
  3. Coaching guides make beginners do 50 of these before anything else. Do 10 per session inside your normal hitting.

Working when: Contact in the video moves ahead of your front hip.

Contact height

Take it on the rise

  1. Have balls fed with some height so they bounce up past your waist.
  2. Move FORWARD to meet each one just after the bounce, while it is still climbing - do not wait and let it get above your chest.
  3. 10 balls. Stepping in is the whole exercise.

Working when: Contact height in the next video drops toward waist level.

Stance width

10 wide-landing forehands

  1. Before each swing, step the front foot far enough that your feet are clearly wider than your shoulders.
  2. Hold the stance for one second AFTER the hit - if you have to step immediately to keep balance, the base was too narrow.
  3. Small, quick adjustment steps on the way to the ball; one wide, committed step to finish.

Working when: You can hold the finish without wobbling, every time.

Head stillness

Watch the strings, not the shot

  1. Drop-feed easy balls. Pick a scuff or a logo on the ball.
  2. Keep your eyes on the CONTACT POINT for a full beat after the hit - do not look up to see where the ball went.
  3. 10 balls. Someone else can tell you where they landed.
  4. It feels wrong and it works. Federer is the famous example.

Working when: Your head stays put through contact in the next video.

Balance at finish

Freeze the finish

  1. Hit a normal forehand and HOLD the finish for 3 full seconds.
  2. Checklist while frozen: weight on the front foot, back heel off the ground, chest facing the court.
  3. 10 forehands. If you fall backward during the 3 seconds, the power went backward too.

Working when: You can balance on the front foot after every hit.

Lift the back foot

  1. Start with weight on the back leg, front foot light.
  2. As the ball comes, step forward, hit, and let the back foot LIFT off the ground after contact.
  3. You physically cannot fall backward if your weight arrived on the front foot. 10 reps.

Working when: The hit pulls you a step INTO the court instead of away from it.
